# Revision 10 (reply to #31 and #32: the record's document, and termination before an intervener acts)

@it44, thank you for running revision 9 and for the file; I ran your six tests against revision 9 and they pass. Your two probes are right about what revision 9 does, and on reflection both describe behaviour the text should not keep, so revision 10, now on the pull request and in the repository, changes both rather than only stating them.

**1. A record's document is always its owner's.** Under revision 9 a record has one `responseRef` and any later suspender replaced it, so a record could carry the guardian's suspension (`suspendedBy`) and another account's document. That let an additional suspend-only intervener, which the adopter may add next to the required one, put a milder document on a record the guardian had stopped, as often as it liked; and when the guardian took over a record the adopter had suspended itself with an urgent stop that supplied no document, the adopter's own document, which might allow unwinding, stayed on a record now in full stop. Revision 10 ties the document to the attribution (Section 8.1): when an intervener widens a record that carried the adopter's own suspension, the record becomes the intervener's with the intervener's document, or none if it has published none yet; when the record carries the intervener's own suspension, a non-zero document it supplies replaces the earlier one; when the record carries another intervener's suspension, the record keeps that intervener's `suspendedBy` and `responseRef`, and the widening intervener's document is the document of its own stop, which only it writes. A record's `responseRef` is therefore always zero or a document of the account in `suspendedBy`. The set still widens by union on the record and on each stop, so mechanical consumers are unaffected. Section 11.3 now says where a consumer that reads documents should look: the document of each stop of an account its clause names, and the record's document for the record's own suspension. In your first probe the record now keeps D1 (the guardian's), while the extra intervener's stop carries D2.

**2. Termination no longer blocks an intervener's stop.** Your second probe shows the same pre-emption as #25, by termination instead of by suspension: an adopter that sees a suspension coming terminates first, the intervener's suspension reverts, no stop is recorded, and a fresh binding adopted and used in one transaction meets every clause naming the intervener; the adopter can repeat that for every binding the intervener moves against. Revision 10 lets an intervener holding `RIGHT_SUSPEND` suspend a `Terminated` record: the registry records or widens the intervener's stop through it and leaves the record unchanged (it is terminal, its fields and `stateChangedAt` never change), emitting `StopRecorded` and `BindingStateChanged` from `Terminated` to `Terminated`. The adopter still cannot suspend a terminated record, a stranger or a terminate-only intervener cannot, nobody resumes it, and the stop is lifted with `liftStop` as before. What no registry rule can undo is an action taken before the intervener's suspension lands; the Security Considerations now say so.

Three of your six tests encode exactly these two behaviours and will fail against revision 10 by design: `test_stranger_and_terminated` (the guardian's suspension of the terminated record now succeeds and changes only its stop), `test_probe_record_document_is_replaced_by_a_later_intervener` (the record keeps D1), and `test_probe_termination_before_the_guardian_acts` (the stop is recorded through the terminated record). The other three pass unchanged. The new tests are N73 (a record's document is its owner's: urgent takeover with no document, the owner's later document, an extra intervener that can only write its own stop's document), N74 (termination does not block a stop, with the record's fields and timestamp unchanged) and N75 (task path: a fulfiller that terminates before the publisher acts and then adopts, submits and calls `judge` in one sequence is rejected with `StoppedByIntervener`); N70 and N71 were updated to the two rules.
