# Revision 2 (reply to be posted in the discussion thread)

Revision 2 of the draft is now on the pull request (https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/2113) and in the repository (https://github.com/garyyang-finchip/agent-safety-framework). The first post has been updated to the new text; this reply lists what changed and why, so that earlier readers can see the delta.

## What changed

**1. Suspension restrictions are a combinable set, with a structured response document.** Revision 1 had five mutually exclusive restrictions. A first round of review made the point that the five are not alternatives: "no new risk" is a prohibition, "shrink" a capability change, "essential only" a duty, "de-risk only" a permitted path and "full stop" a stop target, and a real intervention usually combines them (stop new exposure, keep repayment open, keep the service alive). `suspend` now takes a `uint8` bit set (`RESTRICT_NO_NEW_RISK` 1, `RESTRICT_SHRINK` 2, `RESTRICT_ESSENTIAL_ONLY` 4, `RESTRICT_DERISK_ONLY` 8, `RESTRICT_FULL_STOP` 16; full stop dominates; empty and out-of-range sets are rejected) together with a `responseRef`, the canonical hash of an intervention response document (new schema `intervention-response.schema.json`) that states who started the response and under which right, what it covers, what it prohibits, allows and keeps running, how actions already authorized or sent are handled, how long it lasts and who may end it. `checkBinding` returns the bit set with `Suspended` and with `ParentNotSatisfied`, so a consumer learns what still may proceed without fetching the document. The reference guarded account keeps de-risking open while `RESTRICT_DERISK_ONLY` is set and `RESTRICT_FULL_STOP` is not. This changes the `IASFBindingRegistry` interface identifier to `0xb99bdc2a`; the specification registry's identifier is unchanged.

**2. The fifth protected domain is human and public interests, with protected roles.** `third-party-public` was too narrow: the principal's own rights and welfare (not only their assets) are a protected interest too, and so are the operator's. The domain is now `human-public`, and every `protects` entry names the roles whose interest it serves: `principal`, `operator`, `counterparty`, `bystander`, `public`.

**3. Specifications state permitted and prohibited uses and four more remedies.** `applicability.permittedUses` / `prohibitedUses` and `remedies.reversibility`, `interventionWindow`, `serviceContinuity`, `incidentReporting` were added as optional members. Section 11.8 now separates three facts that must never be inferred from one another: that an account is who it claims to be (identity), that it may act for a principal or resource (representation), and that a given action serves a permitted use (legitimate use). An action can be authentic and authorized and still outside the permitted uses.

**4. Native results of external mechanisms are preserved.** The question of revision 1 ("should the kernel normalize denial reasons of external policy mechanisms?") is closed in the negative. The registry's `Verdict` and `Reason` describe the registry's own evaluation only; when a consumer also reads a policy verdict, a mandate check or an account validation result, that mechanism's native result is kept as it is (Section 10.6). Normalizing them would lose information and would couple the framework to each mechanism's revision cycle.

**5. Incident reports, claims and labels have no power of their own.** Section 11.7 fixes the consumer rules: a report, a claim or a label changes nothing by itself; only the rules of the consumer that reads it give it an effect; statement, acceptance, finding and action are kept apart; corrections are indexed with accusations; report contents are untrusted input. This also answers the fourth question of revision 1: incident and claim records stay outside the kernel in this version.

**6. Analysis basis and design memo.** The repository now carries the design memo (`docs/design-memo.md`) with the three-axis map used as a multi-label index (seven links of an agent system, five protected domains, six sources of failure; eight requirement families as the grouping of profiles), the object-and-authority attribution table (which object settles which question, under whose authority), a fourteen-scenario walkthrough against the registry behaviour and the test suite, and the overlap and adaptation review against the neighbouring proposals. Two earlier statements were withdrawn in that review: the framework does not claim that on-chain enforcement is the only enforcement that counts (the `self`, `runtime` and `custodian` locations exist for that reason), and pricing or deposits are not a condition of the framework's effectiveness.

**7. Smaller corrections.** Examples carry two intervention response documents; vectors carry the response requirement bits; the test suite has 74 tests, 48 of them negative conformance scenarios (N01–N48); the evidence script was re-rehearsed on Anvil with the response documents (36 transactions).

## What did not change

The kernel: two objects (Safety Specification, Safety Binding), one check, one composition rule; the adopter is always `msg.sender`; identifiers and their derivations (Section 3) are unchanged; derived bindings never widen their parent; `Unknown` never satisfies; the text requires only ERC-165.
