# Revision 9 (reply to #25: an intervener's suspension is never blocked)

@it44, thank you: the sequence is exactly as you describe and I reproduced it against the current code. A record carries one suspension, only an `Active` record could be suspended, so an adopter that suspended its own binding first with a mild set left a suspend-only intervener with an invalid transition on `suspend` and an unauthorized `resume`, and the record stayed at the adopter's set, with no stop ever recorded for a clause to rely on. You are right that it is a write-path gap and that revision 8 does not reach it: the set in force can only carry a stop that exists. The same move was open to an additional suspend-only intervener of the adopter's choosing, which the closed resume right of Section 10.2 deliberately allows next to the required one, so "additional suspenders are harmless" was not true either.

Revision 9 is on the pull request and in the repository. An intervener's suspension is never blocked by an existing one (Section 8.1): an intervener holding `RIGHT_SUSPEND` may suspend a record that is already `Suspended`, and the registry widens the record instead of reverting. The record's set becomes the union of the two sets, its `responseRef` is replaced when the intervener supplies a non-zero one, and when the record carried the adopter's own suspension it becomes the intervener's (`suspendedBy` is set to the intervener; a record carrying another intervener's suspension keeps that one). The intervener's standing stop is recorded or widened as for any of its suspensions, and the transition is emitted as `BindingStateChanged` from `Suspended` to `Suspended` with the record's set and document after the widening. The adopter's own re-suspension stays an invalid transition, since the adopter can resume its own suspension and suspend again, which an intervener often cannot. The adopter keeps what it had, the right to suspend an `Active` record and to terminate; what it loses is the ability to hold a record at a weaker set than an intervener demands.

Your four cases are the new tests. N70: the suspend-only guardian escalates to a full stop in one call on a record the adopter suspended with de-risk only (`StopRecorded`, union 24, the guardian's document, `suspendedBy` = guardian); the adopter can no longer resume and still cannot re-suspend; the guardian can widen again, a zero document keeps the earlier one; a guardian with the resume right recovers the same way, in one call rather than two, so the race you noted is gone, and its resumption lifts its stop. N71: an additional suspend-only intervener of the adopter's suspends first with a mild set; the required intervener's widening keeps the record with the first intervener, records the required intervener's own stop, and its later resumption reopens the record without lifting either stop, each intervener lifting its own through `liftStop`. N72, on the task path: a fulfiller that suspends its own binding before the suspend-only publisher acts does not lock the publisher out; judgment rejects with the set in force, full stop included, terminate-and-readopt is still `StoppedByIntervener`, and the publisher's `liftStop` admits the fresh binding.

Text changes: Section 8.1 (the `Suspended -> Suspended` transition and the rule), Section 8.5 (a widening records the stop like any suspension; a resumption lifts a stop only when the record carries that intervener's suspension, otherwise `liftStop`), a Rationale paragraph, a Security Considerations paragraph on pre-emption, and the test cases. No interface or encoding change: `IASFBindingRegistry` stays `0xcff7f826`, the clause encoding and the `requirementId` values are unchanged; the implementation change is confined to `ASFLifecycle`. 98 tests, 72 negative.
