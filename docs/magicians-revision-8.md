# Revision 8 (reply to the thread: the set in force, widening documents, the boundary)

Revision 8 is on the pull request and in the repository. It delivers the three changes promised in #17 and #19, and the sentence promised in #15.

**1. The response set in force carries the stops the clause relies on.** The check still reports the first failing step, but the set it returns with that reason is now defined for the clause, not for the record: the union of the suspended records on the chain and of the standing stops of the accounts the clause names, returned with `Suspended`, `ParentNotSatisfied` and `StoppedByIntervener` alike, and empty when the chain is not in force (Section 10.1). The reason tells a consumer what failed first; the set tells it what is in force, from every source the clause relies on, in one call. @ted0x, your case is N68 and N69: with the guardian's full stop standing through a binding the adopter terminated, a sibling the adopter suspended itself with de-risk only reports `Suspended` with `16 | 8`, and the reference account refuses the repayment it would have let through on the record's own set. Your extra `stopOf` query is no longer needed for correctness; it stays a valid stricter reading, and `stopOf` per named intervener gives the same answer as the set. One earlier assertion changed with the rule: a derived binding under a suspended root, whose adopter is stopped, now reports the stop with the stop's set and the parent's together (N64).

**2. Documents that widen a stop (Section 8.5).** @SergeevDmitry, the text now says what the stop's single `responseRef` is and what a widening document owes. The stop's `responseRef` is the document of its latest recording and is the current reference for the stop as widened; a response document supplied with a suspension that widens a standing stop MUST state the requirements of the widened stop, in full or by naming, in its new `supersedes` member, the earlier documents it incorporates; a zero `responseRef` leaves the earlier document as the reference. The bit set is what the registry's consumers apply and it only widens; the documents carry the detail (`scope`, `mustContinue`, `inFlight`) and must not grow weaker while the set grows stricter. Every document supplied stays on the record it was supplied with and in that recording's `StopRecorded` event, so the sequence is always reconstructible; N65 now asserts that. The response document schema gained `supersedes`, and the examples gained a widening document (`guardian-full-stop-widening.response.json`, bits 25, superseding the guardian's de-risk-only document).

**3. The boundary.** Sections 8.5 and 11.3 now say what #15 said: a suspension or a standing stop reaches the subject's own actions under the binding, not the rights that counterparties and third parties hold over their own assets and positions under the consumer's rules (a buyer's cancellation or acceptance, a refund at a deadline, a third party's clean-up of an expired reservation), which are checked against those rules and continue under any stop unless the rules themselves say otherwise.

No interface or encoding change: `IASFBindingRegistry` stays `0xcff7f826`, the clause encoding and the `requirementId` values are unchanged. The change in the reference implementation is confined to the evaluation library (`ASFEvaluation`). 95 tests, 69 negative. The evidence script gained a seventh transaction phase for the set rule (the guarded account's requirement now names the guardian; the guardian widens its stop through a sibling with the superseding document and repayment is refused although the presented record shows de-risk only; a self-suspension with de-risk only is refused the same way; the lift reopens de-risking under it), rehearsed on Anvil; the third Sepolia run, with the revision-8 contracts, follows and will be recorded next to the first two. The revision-7 deployment stays as it was.
