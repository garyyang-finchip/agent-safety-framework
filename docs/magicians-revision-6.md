# Revision 6 (reply to be posted under the third review)

Both paths are real, and the second one also exposed a contradiction between the text and the code: the security considerations said that a terminated record stays readable with its `suspendedBy`, while `terminate` cleared it. The root is the same in both cases: every rule attached to one `bindingId`, and `adopt` takes a salt, so the adopter decides how many records exist for the same subject and specification and which of them a consumer sees first. A permissionless `judge` then runs before the publisher can react.

Revision 6 makes the stop apply to the adopter, as you put it, and does so in the registry rather than in the adapter. A suspension recorded by an intervener is now also a standing stop, keyed by the adopter, the binding's subject and specification, and the intervener; it carries the binding it was recorded through, the response set and the `responseRef`. It outlives the termination of that binding. It is lifted only by a `resume` of that binding under the existing rights — an account holding `RIGHT_RESUME` on it, never the adopter — and `resume` of a terminated record whose stop still stands lifts the stop, leaves the record `Terminated` and emits `StopLifted`. In the check, a clause that names interveners now fails with a new reason, `StoppedByIntervener`, for any binding of that adopter for that subject and specification while a stop by an account the clause names stands, whatever the binding's own state, and returns the stop's response set; a clause that names nobody is not concerned. `terminate` keeps `suspendedBy`, the response set and the `responseRef` on the record, so the stored state shows who stopped what, not only the events. `stopOf` exposes the standing stop.

I kept the stop keyed rather than scanned. A rule that scanned the adopter's index would have to bound the scan, and a bound is a bypass (push the stopped record outside the window); a rule keyed by what a clause can pin — adopter, subject, specification — reaches exactly the bindings the clause would otherwise accept, at one storage read per named intervener. Keying it per intervener keeps the stop within the clauses that name its author and keeps lifting where the binding put it: the author, or whoever the binding granted the resume right. The adapter's logic is unchanged; it scans the fulfiller's bindings as before and now finds every sibling and every fresh adoption refused. One consequence is stated in the text: a stop by a suspend-only intervener stands until the requirement itself changes, so a requirement that names a suspender should also name who may lift its stops. The tender example now names the publisher with both the suspend and the resume right.

Tests N59 to N64: at the registry level (the adopter's own suspension records no stop; the intervener's does; the stop survives the adopter's termination, cannot be lifted by the adopter or a stranger, is lifted by the guardian through the terminated record; a sibling binding and a fresh binding fail the naming clause with the stop's set and pass a clause that names nobody; the key is per intervener, subject and specification; the stop is checked after the required interveners and before the ancestors), on the task path (your two scenarios verbatim — the sibling and the terminate-and-readopt with `judge` called by the fulfiller — both rejected; with the publisher named with both rights, the publisher lifts through the terminated record and the fresh binding is admitted and paid) and on the wallet path (the principal who terminates the guardian-stopped binding and presents a fresh one is still held to the guardian's de-risk-only set). The suite is at 90 tests, 64 negative. The interface identifier changes with `stopOf` (`IASFBindingRegistry` is now `0x2db6eb8a`); the clause encoding and `requirementId` values are unchanged. Text changes in Sections 6.5, 7, 8.1, a new 8.5, 10.1, 10.2, 11.2, 11.3, the rationale, the security considerations on termination and on intervener key compromise, and the test cases. The registry's writes moved into a second linked library to stay well under Sepolia's per-transaction gas cap; the Sepolia deployment recorded in the repository is revision 5's, and revision 6 will be deployed in a second run. The pull request and the repository carry revision 6; the first post is updated.

Two choices I would like your view on: the key of the stop (adopter, subject, specification, intervener), which makes a stop span scopes and versions but not other subjects of the same adopter; and lifting through the recorded binding's resume right rather than through a separate right of the intervener who stopped.
