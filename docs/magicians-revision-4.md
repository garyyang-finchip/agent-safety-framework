# Revision 4 (reply to be posted under the second review)

You are right, and the case is exact: `adopt` takes any intervener the adopter names, the clause only asked that its own accounts hold at least their rights, and `resume` only refused the adopter's address. A fulfiller names the publisher with suspend, adds a key of its own with `RIGHT_RESUME`, and the publisher's stop lasts one block. The wildcard had the same flaw for the same reason: nothing an adopter chooses can establish independence from the adopter.

Revision 4 does what you suggest, and nothing else. A clause that names interveners now also closes the resume right: the binding must not grant `RIGHT_RESUME` to any account the clause does not name, otherwise `IntervenerMismatch`. Additional interveners holding only suspend or terminate rights remain allowed, since they can only stop. A named account may hold more rights than the clause requires. The zero-account wildcard is gone; a zero account in a clause is a malformed requirement. The escalation shape is expressed where it belongs, in the clause: name the publisher with suspend and, if wanted, an executor with resume, and the binding must grant exactly those resume holders. The registry-level rule from revision 3 stays (an intervener's suspension is never the adopter's to lift); the clause now decides who else may lift it. A clause that names no interveners constrains nothing about the lifecycle, and the text now says plainly that such a subject controls its own.

Tests: N57 at the registry level (the puppet-key binding fails the clause before any suspension; unnamed suspend-only or terminate-only interveners pass; the suspender-plus-executor clause is met by a binding granting exactly that, the executor lifts the suspender's stop and the adopter cannot; a clause without interveners leaves the resume right unconstrained) and N58 on the task path (your scenario verbatim, rejected with `IntervenerMismatch` at judgment; then the escalation variant, where the executor lifts the publisher's stop and the fulfiller is accepted). The suite is at 84 tests, 58 negative. No interface or encoding change: `IASFBindingRegistry` stays `0x1ff33bef` and `requirementId` values are unchanged. Text changes in Sections 6.5, 8.1, 10.1, 10.2, 10.4, 11.2, the rationale and a new security consideration on interveners chosen by the adopter. The pull request and the repository carry revision 4; the first post is updated.

One thing the registry still cannot do is know who controls an account, so the rule is stated as it is: independence comes from the requirement naming accounts, not from any property of the binding. If you see a remaining path by which the adopter lifts a stop under a clause that names its interveners, I would like to hear it.
