# Revision 7 (reply to be posted under the fourth review)

Both points stand, and the second one is the sharper of the two: the reach of a stop came from the clause that named the intervener, while the authority to lift it came from the resume holders of one binding, chosen by the adopter and possibly never named by the clause that relied on the stop. Your two-tender example is now a test (N67), and so is the overwrite: one record per key, replaced on each suspension, let a milder set displace a stricter one and moved the lifting to the newer binding's resume holders (N65, N66).

Revision 7 makes the stop the intervener's. It is lifted only by the intervener that recorded it, or by the one account the intervener names as its lifter (`setStopLifter`, which it may call or change at any time and which applies to all of its stops), either through `liftStop` or through that account's own `resume` of a binding the intervener suspended. Any other holder of the resume right reopens the record and nothing more; no right of the adopter lifts a stop; and a `Terminated` record accepts no `resume` at all, which removes the revision-6 oddity of resuming a terminated record to reach the stop behind it. A later suspension by the same intervener under the same key widens the stop instead of replacing it: the response set becomes the union, the binding identifier moves to the latest suspension, the response document is replaced only when the new suspension supplies one, and the time is that of the first recording. `StopRecorded` states the stop after every recording, `StopLifted` is keyed by the stop rather than by a binding, and `stopLifterOf` reports the lifter.

On "an account it names when it stops": I made the lifter one account per intervener rather than a parameter of each suspension. The intervener makes that choice rarely and should be able to change it while a stop stands, which a value frozen at suspension time would not allow, and a per-suspension parameter would sit on every `suspend`, including the adopter's own, which record no stop. If you see a case that needs a different lifter per stop, I would add it as a second, optional path rather than replace this one. The escalation shape survives at both levels: the clause names the executor with the resume right for the record, and the suspender names the executor as its lifter for the stop; N57 and N58 now show the executor's resumption alone leaving the publisher's stop in place. One consequence is stated in the security considerations: a compromised intervener key can rename the lifter, so the lifter is a review path, not a recovery path, and a consumer whose intervener key is lost changes its requirement.

The interface identifier of `IASFBindingRegistry` is now `0xcff7f826`; the clause encoding and the `requirementId` values are unchanged. Text changes in Sections 6.5, 7, 8.1, 8.5, 10.2 and 11.2, a new rationale paragraph on who lifts a stop, the security considerations on intervener key compromise and on termination and sibling bindings, and the test cases. 93 tests, 67 negative. The pull request and the repository carry revision 7; the first post is updated. Nothing of revision 6 was deployed, so the second Sepolia run will deploy revision 7.
